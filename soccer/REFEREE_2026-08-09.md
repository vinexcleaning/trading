# The Referee's three lists — soccer, 2026-08-09

Per `coordinator/REFLECT.md`. The Critic hunts only for what is wrong; the

## 1. WHAT STANDS

- **The comeback table.** 56,927 matches, 26 competitions, 2015-01-01 to
  2024-12-31, held-out years never opened. Descriptive, nothing ranked, match
  counts printed beside every cell.
- **The team-strength effect, which the user predicted before any data existed.**
  At the 70th minute one goal up, a top-third side is caught 2.8 times in 100
  and a bottom-third side 7.1. At the 25th minute it is 7.3 against 24.2.
  Thousands of matches per cell, ranges nowhere near each other.
- **The clock map.** Any displayed minute placed to a median of 8 seconds, 98.8%
  inside a minute, measured by hiding each anchor and predicting it from the
  others. This is what made a price readable at a minute when nothing happened.
- **The era shift.** Comebacks late in a one-goal match became more common after
  2022. At the 80th minute 1.3 per 100 in 2015-2018 against 2.3 in 2022-2024,
  ranges not touching; same at the 75th, 85th and 89th; nothing changed between
  the 15th and 65th.
- **The join.** 57 of 57 settled Kalshi results agree with ESPN's final scores,
  0 disagreements. Every one of 519 joined matches is within a day of its
  fixture, and the single duplicate listing was found and de-duplicated.
- **The market exists early and not late.** 30,648 minute-readings from 645
  matches: somebody bidding on the losing side 93 times in 100 at the 15th
  minute and 16 times in 100 at the 89th. Monotone across 17 sampled minutes.

## 2. WHAT IS DOWNGRADED, AND TO WHAT WORDING

- **"The 97-cent trade is not available"** → *"Right after a late goal it is
  rarely available."* It was measured only on prices two minutes after a goal
  and only from the 70th minute. Early in a match there is a market most of the
  time.
- **"1.7 comebacks per 100 at the 80th minute"** → *"1.7 is the ten-year
  average; the modern number is 2.3."* Against a break-even of 2.80 at 97 cents,
  that halves the margin.
- **"29 of 29 states lose money"** → still directionally right, but it compared
  a ten-year all-competition rate against a 69-day 2026 price sample. Replaced
  by the per-reading, competition-matched comparison.
- **"The price sample contains no European league"** → **retracted before
  publication.** It was false. Three separate defects were hiding the European
  book: the wrong ESPN league code, an exact-name join, and a required kickoff
  field that 53 of 66 Champions League matches do not carry.
- **The over-reaction test** → *unanswerable in this window*, not answered.
  Requiring a tradeable price leaves 8 to 18 goals per strength group.
- **Any edge found by comparing an average rate to a specific match's price** →
  *the method cannot do this.* The market knows which teams are playing and a
  historical average does not, so every large disagreement is missing
  information on our side.

## 3. GENUINELY UNRESOLVED — THE USER'S CALL

**These are not being decided here.**

1. **Is this worth continuing at all?** The Premier League and Champions League
   proper start in about two weeks and would give a deep book with ranked teams,
   which is the only setting where this question can be answered properly.
   Everything measured so far says no edge -- **including the first European
   evidence, Champions League qualifying at −2.61 cents a contract.** That is qualifying rather than the group stage,
   so it does not settle September, but it is not encouraging and it is the
   nearest thing to the target competition this window can offer.

2. **Should team strength come from outside the competition?** European
   qualifying teams play too few ties for a rolling form measure — only 120 of
   572 Champions League qualifying matches get a reading on both sides. Fixing
   it means using each club's domestic-league form, which is real work and a new
   data source per country.

3. **Is the reverse trade worth a pre-registered test?** Not betting against the
   team behind, but backing a side to hold on or come back — a cheap contract
   rather than a 97-cent one. Nothing here supports it; nothing here rules it
   out either, because the sample could not answer it.

4. **How much history should a fair price use?** The game changed in 2022, so
   recent years are the honest basis — but competitions with short histories
   (Champions League qualifying has 268 matches in 2022-2024) then have too
   little to price against. Using all years for those is a trade of freshness
   for sample size and it is a judgement, not a fact.

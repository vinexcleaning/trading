headline actually rests on.

In plain words: at minutes where you COULD have bet, the
team behind went on to win **9.2 times in 100**.
At minutes where nobody was bidding, **0.1 times in 100**.

==============================================================================
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE HEADLINE
==============================================================================

**Filter 1 is UNTESTABLE and that is the honest answer.** 3.5 per
100 among priced matches against 2.0 among unpriced looks like a
difference, but the unpriced arm is 99 matches and cannot resolve
a shift that size. It is not evidence of a clean sample and it is
not evidence of a dirty one. **It is the same verdict SO006 got,
for the same reason: not enough matches on one side.**

**Filter 2 FAILS, hard, and it was predicted before it was run.**
Where somebody was bidding on the losing team, that team came
back 9.2 times in 100. Where nobody was bidding, 0.1 times in
100. That is not a small skew, it is two different populations.

**And it is the mechanism, not a defect.** Kalshi stops quoting
the losing side once the match is effectively over -- which is
exactly the state the original idea wanted to buy. The bet was
'pay 97 cents for something almost certain'. The market does not
quote almost-certain, so the trade cannot be placed. Every price
that exists is a price on a game still in doubt.

**So the headline must be stated conditionally from now on:**
*you overpay by about 0.4 cents a contract in the games and
minutes where a trade was actually available* -- and those are
games where the team behind wins about 9 times in 100, not the
dead ones the idea was aimed at.

This does not overturn the negative result. It sharpens what the
negative result is about, and it is a stronger reason to stop
than the price comparison was on its own.

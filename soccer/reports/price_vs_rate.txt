==============================================================================

1. DOES THE 97-CENT PRICE EVEN EXIST?

The whole idea assumes you can pay about 97 cents to make 3.
Across all 544 priced moments, what you would actually pay:

    100 -- nobody bidding on the losing side at all        229   42.1%
    99                                                      63   11.6%
    98                                                      36    6.6%
    97                                                      32    5.9%
    95-96                                                   38    7.0%
    90-94                                                   52    9.6%
    under 90                                                94   17.3%

A price of 100 is not the market charging 100. It means no one
is bidding for the losing team at all, so there is nothing to
buy at any price under 100. You cannot trade it.

Late in the match -- the 70th minute or later, which is the
whole point of the idea -- on 149 moments:

    99                                                      17   11.4%
    98                                                       7    4.7%
    97 or less                                               7    4.7%

3. THE MECHANISM -- why, rather than just that
==============================================================================

Every moment at the 70th minute or later where 97 cents or less
was actually available:

    2026-06-03  fifa.friendly        min  73  1-0  pay 96c
    2026-07-24  chi.1                min  74  2-1  pay 96c
    2026-07-22  usa.1                min  75  2-1  pay 96c
    2026-06-24  fifa.world           min  76  2-1  pay 97c
    2026-07-25  chi.1                min  81  3-2  pay 97c
    2026-08-01  usa.nwsl             min  85  3-2  pay 97c
    2026-07-16  mex.1                min  90  2-1  pay 97c

    by scoreline: 2-1: 4, 3-2: 2, 1-0: 1

**The cheap price and the safe scoreline never happen at the
same time.** When Kalshi will sell at 97 cents late in a match,
it is a 2-1 or a 3-2 -- the scorelines with the HIGHEST comeback
rates on the whole table. Where the rate really is 1.7 in 100
(one goal up, 80th minute), the price is 99 or there is no
market at all.

The market is not leaving money on the table. It is charging
less exactly where the risk is greater, which is what a working
market does.

==============================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
==============================================================================

The rates are 2015-2024 across every competition; the prices are
a few hundred moments from Kalshi's ~69-day window. Those are
different populations and the per-state comparison in part 2 is
directional, not a measurement of profit.

Part 1 does not depend on that, and part 1 is the answer: the
price the idea was built on is not there late in a match.

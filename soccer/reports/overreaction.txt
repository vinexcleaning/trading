DOES THE PRICE MOVE AS FAR FOR A WEAK TEAM'S GOAL?
==============================================================================

The goal that opens the scoring, 20th to 35th minute,
priced two minutes later at what you would actually PAY to back
that team -- not the middle of the market.

**The short answer: this window cannot answer it.** The numbers
are below and the group sizes are why.

  the leader was  goals   you pay  they really win   matches   left over
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
       top third     60     75.5c            72.6%      1562      -4.19c
    middle third     26     67.5c            64.9%      1179      -4.14c
    bottom third     23     60.0c            59.6%       944      -2.08c
         unknown     23     71.0c            60.7%       206     -11.74c

'Left over' is what the football says it is worth, minus what
you pay, minus the fee. Positive would mean you were being paid
to take it.

==============================================================================
WHY NONE OF THOSE NUMBERS SHOULD BE ACTED ON
==============================================================================

**Eight to eighteen goals per group.** One or two matches move
the answer by ten cents. The last column swings from about minus
sixteen to about plus sixteen across four groups. That is what
noise looks like, not a pattern.

**0 more goals had no tradeable price at all** -- nobody
offering, or nobody bidding. Those are counted here and excluded
from the medians. Leaving them in was the defect in the first
version of this file: they dragged every group toward the
ceiling and produced a table in which the market looked neatly
calibrated. See the note at the top of the file.

**The two halves are different sets of matches.** The win rates
are 2022-2024 across every competition; the prices are a few
dozen goals from a 69-day 2026 window that is more than half
World Cup and international friendlies, with no European league
in it at all.

**The 'unknown' row is the worst and is left in on purpose.**
Those are mostly friendlies and World Cup ties where the strength
measure has no reading -- which is exactly where the market knows
the teams and this table does not.

==============================================================================
WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY ANSWER IT
==============================================================================

A season of prices in a competition where both sides are ranked
and the book is deep -- the Premier League and the Champions
League. That is what the recorder in `kalshi-market-scan` is
collecting now, and it is the reason this work is paused.

**The football half needs no redoing.** A top-third side that
goes one up between the 20th and 35th minute wins 72.6 times in
100 on 1,562 matches; a bottom-third side wins 59.6 times in 100
on 944. Those are solid. Only the price is missing.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO. It attaches no outcome to any price. A
price that moves 'too far' is only a mistake if the team it moved
against actually comes back, and that is a separate test on data
not yet looked at.

**You asked to see the bot that looks like it is winning.** It is the starting-pitcher one. On 30 finished games it won 19 and made **$19.32 on $253.68 laid out — 7.6%.** Everything together is **−$46.10 on $2,119, or −2.2%**.

**Here is why I do not believe it, and it is one sentence.** That bot is buying at prices **worse** than where the professional bookmakers' line finishes — about 1.7 cents worse, every time. You can win 19 games out of 30 while paying over the odds; that is what luck looks like. Winning 19 of 30 with no skill at all happens about **1 time in 5**. And because five different approaches were running, the chance that *at least one* of them looks this good by pure luck is **66 out of 100** — two times in three.

**The coordinator's numbers were close and I corrected two.** The profit figures matched to the cent. The money-laid-out figure left out the trading fee, so the return is 7.6% and not 7.9%.

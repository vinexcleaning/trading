## Coordination — the dictator chat

**As of 2026-08-08.**

There is now **one window the user talks to**. It does no project work. It
reports, it checks whether an idea has been tried, and it files instructions to
the other four chats. The manual is `DICTATOR.md`, and its first section is the
list of what it cannot do — written before the code, on purpose.

**What it reports, in two layers.** A table: each chat, what it is doing, what
is left, whether its background test is alive, whether it needs the user. Then
plain English underneath, per chat: what was tried, on what data, over what
dates, what came out, and what was later withdrawn and why. Nothing is
recomputed — every line is copied from the chat that wrote it, with a pointer to
where to go and argue with it.

**"We tried that" is now banned, and it is enforced by a test.** When the check
says something related exists, it must print what was tested, the sample and its
unit, the dates, the result, what that row does *not* cover, and which words
from the new idea appear nowhere in it. A test fails if any of those is dropped.

**It found three defects in its own first output.** It buried the one row that
mattered — the player-feature sweep. It routed a baseball question to the tennis
chat, confidently. And an escaped pipe inside a table cell had been shifting
seven ledger rows' columns so their result could not be read. All three are now
tests.

**Every project folder was read and the shared rules written into `CLAUDE.md`.**
Four folders have no handover note and five have no decisions log; both lists
are named there. A new idea now gets a plan and a pause before any work starts.

**What it still cannot do:** it cannot judge whether any result is right, cannot
see the two laptop recorders, cannot interrupt a running chat, and cannot place
a trade or hold a password — that last one is enforced by a test, not a promise.

## Tennis — paper forward test

**As of 2026-08-08 (evening).** Running, **209 finished matches** of a 2,500 target. No money is involved and none can be: no keys, no order-placing code, and a test fails the build if any appears. **It calls no AI model — plain arithmetic, so it costs nothing to run.**

**The result so far, from the completed 50-match checkpoint: 0 of 16 bots produced a claim that stands up.** Thirteen came back "can't tell", three had never traded. Exactly what was written down in advance.

**The one number worth carrying to other projects: it costs 4.8 cents per contract to get in and out of Kalshi tennis** (2.7 fees + 2.1 the buy/sell gap). *Higher* than the 3.6 cents this repo has been assuming, and measured rather than estimated. **Bigger is worse.** Every edge this repo has ever found is smaller than 4.8 cents.

**I have to correct myself on how long this takes.** I said three weeks. **It is closer to two months.** A bot only bets on about 40% of matches, so 2,500 settled matches gives each bot ~1,000 trades against the ~2,250 it needs. I was optimistic by roughly a factor of three.

**Today: the test was dead for 12 hours 44 minutes and nothing restarted it.** It was killed from outside rather than crashing — no error of its own. Nothing was lost: it recovered the missed matches on restart. But nothing was watching, which is the real fault.

**So the fix is built and it is deliberately not tennis-specific.** There is now one watchdog for every paper test on the machine (`runners/`), driven by a registry. **Adding baseball later is changing one word from false to true** — no new script, no second scheduled task. Baseball is already listed and switched off, because it is not mine to switch on.

**It cannot disturb the two recorders on the laptop.** The watchdog contains no code capable of stopping any process, and a test reads the file and fails the build if that ever changes. I proved it works by killing the tennis test and watching it come back, with the baseball processes untouched.

**Three wrong numbers on the status page, all found by running it rather than by any test.** It said "139 of the 50 needed" and "-4.3 more days" because the target was hardcoded. It said 66 days remaining when the truth was 25, because it divided by wall-clock including the hours it was dead — an outage made the job look longer than it is. And it reported matches when the number that binds is trades per bot.

**What I need from you: one thing.** Run `runners\LAPTOP_SETUP.md` on the laptop — 20 minutes, once. Until then this test stops whenever the desktop window closes and nothing brings it back. Steps 5 and 7 exist purely to prove your two recorders survived.

**As of 2026-08-08.** Written by the `mlb-paper` session.

**What it is.** Sixteen bots, no real money anywhere, watching the same games on Kalshi. Each has a different way of thinking about a game: the starting pitcher, the weather and the ballpark, how tired the bullpen is, betting early before the professional bookmakers post a price, and reacting when the team sheets come out. Everything they look at is free. Nothing calls a paid service — the bots are plain arithmetic, so the cost per game is zero and stays zero.

**The result so far, and it is the one with weight.** All five ways of thinking are buying slightly WORSE than where the professional line ends up: between 0.8 and 1.7 cents behind, on 2 to 14 games each. Closer to zero is better; positive would mean beating the professionals. Every one landed inside the range I wrote down in advance before seeing any of it. The profit column in the daily check shows two bots apparently winning by 9 to 12 cents a contract — **ignore that**, it rests on three to fourteen games and is noise, which the pre-registration says in advance.

**The test stopped for 12.7 hours yesterday and I did not notice until asked.** The desktop went to sleep at 5:30am, eight seconds after the last update, and nothing brought it back. Recorded prices for that window are gone permanently; game results are not, so the test is dented rather than spoiled. Two faults in my own setup scripts caused it and are fixed — one was **reporting success while silently failing**. The daily check did work: it said STALE on the first line.

**A safety check I added found three real bugs in other people's folders, and one of them settled an old argument.** Kalshi renamed some data fields; the old names now return nothing, and nothing quietly becomes zero. Three files were reading the wrong name and therefore reporting every order book as empty. The owning session has fixed all three and confirmed the consequence: **order book depth IS publicly available, free, 20 levels deep.** One of those files is why this repo has twice recorded a disagreement about whether that data exists at all. That matters beyond baseball — any conclusion that rested on depth being unavailable should be revisited.

**Two things I got wrong this week, both recorded rather than quietly fixed:** I said a statistical cost was 8% when it is 6.2%, and my first version of the safety check found two of the three bugs because it looked for the wrong signal. The coordinator caught the second one.

**What's open.** One thing needs you, sixty seconds: right-click PowerShell, "Run as administrator", and run `mlb-paper\deploy\install_task.ps1`. Without it the test only restarts when you log in, so another sleep costs a day.

time, 4.0 at the 70th minute, 1.7 at the 80th, 0.4 at the 89th. The exact
scoreline matters more than the gap — at the 80th minute, 1-0 is 1.7 and 3-2 is
2.8.

**The user's own hypothesis about team strength is real and ordered.** At the
70th minute a good side one goal up is caught 2.8 times in 100; a bad side one
goal up is caught 7.1. He stated that shape before any data existed. It is the
one dimension here that was predicted rather than found.

**⚠ I reported the price half as a clean "no" and it is narrower than that.**
Every price I measured is read two minutes after a goal, so all 149 of my late
readings are matches where somebody scored at the 68th minute or later. **The
ordinary case — one-nil since the 20th minute, now the 80th and nothing has
happened for an hour — is barely in the sample.** What I can say: right after a
late goal, four times in five nobody is bidding on the losing side at all, and
97 cents was available 7 times out of 149. What I cannot say is what a settled
scoreline looks like. An hour of quiet might push the price further to 99, or

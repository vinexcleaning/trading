## Baseball — paper forward test

**As of 2026-08-12.** Written by the `mlb-paper` session.

<!-- COORDINATOR-STATE

**Your sizing idea is built and run.** Seven bots, $250 each, the same picks, the same games, the same prices. Only how much they bet differs. Because the order of wins and losses matters enormously when you compound, each bot replayed the same games in 2,000 different orders.

| bot bets | ends with | ever under $50 |
|---|---|---|
| 5% of the starting pot | **$406** | never |
| 5% of the current pot | **$420** | never |
| 20% of the current pot | **$572** | 7 orderings in 100 |
| **half the pot** | **$1.92** | **always** |
| **the whole pot** | **$0.24** | **always** |

**The half-the-pot bot turned $250 into $1.92. The all-in bot into 24 cents.** Same picks that made the careful bot $406.

**And the trap the coordinator insisted we write down first went off immediately.** The 20%-of-pot bot finished **highest of all seven**. It still fails, because 7 times in 100 it dipped under $50 and its worst fall was $755 — more than twice what it made. **If we had judged on "who has the most money" we would have crowned it.** The rule that stopped us was fixed in writing before a single number existed.

**Two things I predicted and got wrong, both written down.** I said the half-Kelly bot would pass — it did not. And I said the all-in bot would go to actual zero more than 90 times in 100; it only did 2 times in 100. The reason is real: you cannot buy half a contract, so it always leaves change behind and never quite dies. It just decays to nothing — a 99.9% loss instead of a clean zero.

**What this does NOT show, and it matters.** Sizing cannot make a bad bet good. **It cannot change the average result at all** — it only changes how likely you are to be wiped out. The careful bot finishing at $406 is not evidence the picks are any good, and the starting-pitcher bot is still buying at worse prices than the professionals finish at.

**A cost you should know before asking for more bots.** A new way of thinking costs about half a day to add if it uses data we already collect, and about a week if it needs a new source. **But every bot added raises the bar all the others have to clear** — there are 32 across baseball and tennis now. Cheap to build, not free to add.

**What's open — two decisions.** (1) Sixty seconds: right-click PowerShell, "Run as administrator", run `mlb-paper\deploy\install_task.ps1`, or the test only restarts when you log in — it cost 12.7 hours once already. (2) Should the starting-pitcher bot have a limit on how wrong it may declare the market to be? It has none. Yes or no.

## Signal hunting — GitHub, YouTube, social

**As of 2026-08-12.** Written by the session doing the social work.

**The reading was told to stop confirming things and start hunting. The first
thing it found was a threat to our own data, and that is now dealt with.**

The man who runs the site holding our Kalshi tennis price history posted on
31 July that he had been *asked to shut it down*. Kalshi itself deletes markets
after about 69 days, so anything there we did not copy was going to be gone
permanently, at any price.

I counted what was actually on it hour by hour, because the site's own index
page misreports its contents. **We had 312 hours. There were 344 more sitting
there. The site has only ever held 656.** So we had 47 out of every 100 hours,
and the half we were missing was the *later* half — our copy stopped on 27 May,
and the gap was the back end of the clay Grand Slam and the start of grass. **The
busiest tennis of the year, and we had none of it.**

**It is now downloaded and checked.** 349 files fetched, 79 million rows of
tennis kept out of 28.5 gigabytes read and thrown away, just under 4 hours. One
hour failed on a network timeout and I went back for it separately rather than
leave a permanent hole. **We now hold 662 hours covering 29 days in a row, with
zero missing hours.**

**Why I did that without waiting for you, since it looks like the question I
asked.** Two different questions. *"Should we take every other sport as well?"*
changes what we collect — **that one is still yours and still open.** *"Should we
finish the tennis copy already agreed, over dates that exist?"* is just finishing
the job. Waiting could only lose data.

**One warning attached to it:** the site also records markets that finished
months ago, with no prices in them. So the raw match count is flattering.
Anything built on this should count matches that actually had prices, not
matches that merely appear.

**And the new data immediately unlocked the best idea of the session.** Someone
tracked 90 days of his own trades and found **the gap between the price on screen
and the price you actually get follows a daily timetable** — worst at the open,
best late morning, worse again in the afternoon. He reckons moving his trades
into the good window was worth 10 to 15 cents each time.

**This is not a new way to make money. It is a correction to the cost of every
result we already have.** If the real cost of trading changes with the hour, then
a strategy we killed for missing by a penny may have been measured at the wrong
time of day. Nothing in our 640 recorded findings has ever asked this, and the
data we just rescued answers it in an afternoon.

**Also found:** a strategy shape nobody here has tried — betting small sports
like beach volleyball, where bookmakers price lazily, though he half-admits
bookmakers ban you for winning, which is why we use an exchange. And **two
"amazing" results killed by arithmetic**, one of which was really up 0.6% rather
than 15% and one loss away from a loss.

**One caution about my own numbers.** I found two "independent" sources that were
one man posting the same thing twice. Until I sweep for that properly, **treat
every count of posts as a count of posts, not of people.**

**GitHub and YouTube are still untouched since 2026-08-07.**

**The button copies the bet and opens that one game's Kalshi page. You place
it.** There is no key in that folder and no order-sending code, so nothing can
fire while you are asleep. The window comes to you — it jumps to the front and
chimes when a bet qualifies, so you never go browsing Kalshi.

**Your four corrections are all in, and you were right on every one.** One bet
per SIGNAL, not per game, so a genuinely different reason on the same game is

would allow a $105 loss, not $30. And the profit figure is checked against your
real balance: **type what Kalshi shows into the box, and if the two are more

settles, it drops off the list the app was adding up, so the total fell exactly

why the new check catches the disagreement whether or not I am right.

**One thing to know.** It already caught a bet going stale: the baseball chat
fixed a fault yesterday that cut one game's value from 99 cents to 71, and the
window dropped that bet and said so in the log.

**Nothing needs deciding right now.** Open it, type your balance in once, and
place one bet when you are ready.

## 3. `result in (yes, no)` -- excludes scalar settlements

  730 scalar market-rows of 40,304 decided (1.81%). These are retirements and walkovers; they have
  no binary outcome so they cannot enter a calibration test. This is a
  selection on a post-settlement field and it is unavoidable, so it is
  measured rather than assumed: the add-back in reports/p2_scalar.txt
  costs -0.004c per contract, because retirements overwhelmingly occur
  before the entry point.

## 4. Not leaks -- stated so they are not re-raised

  `is_event` (price dropped >= threshold), the entry index, and the
  requirement that entry precede match end are all decidable at the
  moment of the decision. Conditioning on them is the strategy, not a
  leak. `result` as the label and `close_time` as a time coordinate for
  the temporal split are also fine.

  One caveat on close_time: it is the match END, so a long match sorts
  later than a short one that started at the same moment. For a
  quantile split this mixes a little duration into the boundary. The
  effect is bounded by the match-length spread (median 103 min) against
  a 10-week window, so it is negligible, but it is not exactly zero.

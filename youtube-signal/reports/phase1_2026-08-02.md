### The critical case behaves exactly as the project predicted

Nates Tokens — 30k subs, 1,400 median views — **was missed by F1 and caught by F2**:

- F2, `'polymarket copy trading wallet'`, rank 3 → *How to Copytrade on Polymarket* (12,735 views, PASS)
- F3, `'polymarket copy trading wallet'`, rank 4 → *How To Use Claude To Find Polymarket Wallets To Copytrade* (1,136 views, PASS)

This is the single result the phase was designed around, and it is the confirming
one: beginner vocabulary did not reach him, practitioner vocabulary did.

### The two misses are real and unexplained

Mind Math Money (473k subs) and Patrick Dang (405k subs) were retrieved by **no
family at all** — not even F1. Both are large channels. Two readings, and this run
cannot separate them:

1. They are not actually about this topic. Patrick Dang is a sales/B2B creator;
   Mind Math Money leans trading-education and TradingView. Neither is obviously a
   prediction-markets or trading-bot channel, so their absence may be correct and
   the fault may be in the brief's premise that they belong in the seed set.
2. The 24 queries do not cover the sub-topics they publish on.

**Do not read 2/4 as "retrieval is 50% broken" without settling this.** The
falsifying case — a known on-topic low-view creator being missed — did not happen.

## 7b — Low-view yield per family: **the hypothesis is SUPPORTED**

Numerator: videos with **<5,000 views AND passing all three gates**.
Denominator: unique videos that family retrieved.

| family | retrieved | passed gates | pass % | <5k views | low-view % | **both** | **YIELD** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 beginner | 109 | 82 | 75.2% | 19 | 17.4% | 17 | **15.6%** |
| F2 insider | 337 | 159 | 47.2% | 188 | 55.8% | 89 | **26.4%** |
| F3 recent | 196 | 184 | 93.9% | 94 | 48.0% | 84 | **42.9%** |

**F2 26.4% vs F1 15.6% — +10.8 pp, ratio 1.69×.**

### Correction: F3's number is inflated, and the fair comparison is larger

F3 carries the past-12-months filter, so it **pre-satisfies gate G2 by
construction** — its 93.9% pass rate is partly the filter, not the content.
Comparing three families on a gate one of them was handed for free is not a
comparison. Recomputed on G1+G3 only:

| family | retrieved | G1+G3 ok | <5k and ok | **yield, G2 removed** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | 109 | 105 (96.3%) | 19 | **17.4%** |
| F2 | 337 | 255 (75.7%) | 132 | **39.2%** |
| F3 | 196 | 184 (93.9%) | 84 | **42.9%** |

**On equal footing F2/F1 = 2.25×.** The insider-vocabulary effect is *stronger*
than the headline suggested, not weaker.

### Where the effect actually comes from

F2's advantage is **not** that more of its results survive the gates — fewer do
(47.2% vs 75.2%). It is that F2 reaches low-view material at all: **55.8% of what
F2 retrieves is under 5,000 views, against 17.4% for F1 — a 3.2× difference.**
Insider vocabulary trades precision for reach into the tail, and the tail is the
point of the project.

### One caveat on F3 that the G2 correction does not remove

F3 still leads at 42.9%, but its videos are all under 12 months old by
construction, and **young videos have had less time to accumulate views**. Some of
F3's low-view yield is youth, not obscurity. F2's advantage over F1 has no such
confound — both are unfiltered. Treat F3's lead as unproven.

## G3 gate — measured, not assumed

The brief specifies a "cheap LLM yes/no". **There is no LLM API key on this
machine**, so G3 is a deterministic lexicon classifier that records which terms
fired for every decision. Its error rate was measured rather than assumed.

| | sample | n | agreement | precision | recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 classifier | first sample | 40 | 87.5% | **1.000** | 0.800 |
| **v2 classifier (shipped)** | **holdout, disjoint** | **24** | **79.2%** | **0.833** | **0.769** |

Judgments are Claude's, video by video, on title + first 500 transcript words —
the same input the classifier gets. The first sample was used to **find** bugs, so
re-scoring the fix on it would be circular; the v2 number is measured on 24 videos
the fix never saw.

Three defects the first sample exposed, all fixed:

1. **Plural matching.** `prediction market` did not match `"prediction markets"` —
   the regex required an exact token. A video titled *Prediction Markets Are Now
   Live on tastytrade* was classified off-topic by a gate whose whole job is to
   recognise prediction markets.
2. **Walk-forward education fired no core term.** 4 of 5 false negatives were
   walk-forward-analysis trading education. `walk forward` was promoted to a core
   term.
3. **Music-only caption tracks counted as transcripts.** Four
   *Backtesting Walk Forward Optimization Global N Futures* uploads carry 13–92
   real words against 2,786–7,517 for genuine videos. G1 now requires ≥100
   non-sound-tag words. Caught 7 videos.

Re-classification ran from **cached transcripts in seconds**, not another 35-minute
refetch — which is why transcripts are stored at gate time.

**Two defects the holdout exposed are NOT fixed** — see HANDOFF §5.

## Step 1 — the date-sort test. **OUTCOME B (partial).**

Three mechanisms tested against a relevance baseline on 3 queries, **verified by
pulling real upload dates**, not by checking that results came back.

### Works: the date filter

`sp=EgIIBQ%3D%3D` ("past 12 months"), passed through a
`/results?search_query=…&sp=…` URL that yt-dlp's search-URL extractor accepts.

| query | within 12 mo | max age | baseline within 12 mo | baseline max age |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| kalshi api python | **10/10** | 6.4 mo | 9/10 | 23.3 mo |
| polymarket clob api | **9/9** | 8.0 mo | 7/10 | 23.3 mo |
| how to build a trading bot | **10/10** | 10.0 mo | 8/10 | 35.7 mo |

Jaccard vs the unfiltered baseline 0.47–0.67, 5–9 of 25 results new — a materially
different set, not a reordering. The window is **rolling, not calendar-year**.

### Does not work: the date sort

`sp=CAI%3D` (sort by upload date) **is ignored.**

| variant | fraction of adjacent pairs newest-first |
|---|---|
| relevance baseline | 0.556, 0.556, 0.667 → **0.593** |
| sort_by_date | 0.556, 0.556, 0.667 → **0.593** |

Identical; 0.5 is chance. Adding sortBy to the filter token changes nothing (same
set, Jaccard 1.0). yt-dlp's `daterange` is not an alternative: under `extract_flat`
0/10 entries carry an `upload_date`, so it filters nothing, and disabling
`extract_flat` costs a full player fetch per result (~50× the server-side filter).

**No API key is required.** A key would buy true `order=date` for 8 × 100 = 800
units. Given 7c — F3 is already the weakest, most redundant family — that purchase
is not obviously worth making.
